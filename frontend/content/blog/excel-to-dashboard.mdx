Most small teams already have their numbers. They live in a sales sheet someone updates on Fridays, an export from the
accounting tool, or a CSV from the payment provider. What's missing is the hour it takes to turn that into something you
can read at a glance, every week.

This guide takes one ordinary sheet to a dashboard you can share. It assumes no formulas, no pivot tables and no data team.

## 1. Give the sheet one job

A dashboard is only as clear as the table under it. Before you upload anything, check three things.

| Check | Works well | Causes trouble |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Header row | One row of column names at the top | Titles, logos or merged cells above the headers |
| Rows | One row per sale, order or invoice | Subtotals and blank spacer rows mixed in |
| Dates | The same format all the way down | "03/04/2026" in one row and "4 March" in the next |

One more habit pays off: spell labels the same way every time. "Paid", "paid" and "PAID" are counted as three
different values, so a filter or a chart will split them.

<Callout title="Tip: keep the raw data and the report apart">
If your sheet has a summary tab with totals, upload the tab with the individual rows instead. The dashboard does the
summing, and it can't re-slice a total that's already been added up.
</Callout>

## 2. Upload it

Save the tab as CSV or keep it as `.xlsx`, then open **Data** in the sidebar and drop it in. A file like this is all it takes:

```csv
Date,Region,Product,Amount
2026-01-05,North,Pro,100
2026-01-20,South,Pro,50
2026-02-03,North,Setup,200
2026-03-01,West,Setup,300
```

Vyuham shows a preview first and labels each column as a **number**, **date**, **category** or **text**. If a column is
labelled wrongly, say a customer ID read as a number, change it there. Every later chart uses your correction.

## 3. Start from the dashboard it suggests

Once the file is in, go to **Dashboards → New dashboard** and choose **Build it for me**. You get a handful of charts
chosen from the columns themselves, for example:

- the trend over time, from your date column;
- the biggest categories, from region or product;
- a headline number, such as total sales, with the change from the previous period.

Treat it as a first draft. Delete what you don't need, and drag the rest into the order you'd read them.

## 4. Ask for what's missing

Type a question the way you'd ask a colleague:

- "Sales by region last quarter"
- "Top products by sales"
- "Sales by month"

Each answer comes back as a sentence and a chart. If it's useful, click **Save to dashboard**.

## 5. Add filters, then share

Pick one or two columns people will want to slice by, usually region, product or salesperson, and turn them into
dashboard filters. The date range filter is there already.

When it's ready, invite your team as viewers, or export a PDF or Excel copy for the people who live in their inbox.
Next Friday, open the dataset and use **Replace file** with the new export. The dashboard keeps its charts as long as the columns stay the same.

## What to do next

Once one dashboard is running, the next step is noticing changes without having to look. Set an alert on the number that
matters most, like weekly sales dropping below a level, and let the dashboard tell you instead.
