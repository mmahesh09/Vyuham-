Vyuham is a web app for two kinds of work on the same data. You upload an Excel or CSV file, or connect a PostgreSQL or
MySQL database, then type a short question such as "revenue by month". Vyuham returns a chart and a one-line answer, and
you can pin that answer to a dashboard your team can open.

The same workspace holds the work behind those answers: pipelines that clean and join your tables, a SQL Workbench, dynamic
tables that refresh themselves, and rules about who can see which columns.

It's built for growing companies where some people need the numbers and someone prepares them. Often that's a founder, an
owner or an operations manager on one side and an analyst or engineer on the other. In a small team it can be the same
person.

You can [try it with sample data](/try) without signing up. The rest of this post explains how it works, where it fits and
what it can't do yet.

## What does the name Vyuham mean?

Vyuham comes from the Sanskrit word *vyūha*, which means a formation or arrangement. In the Mahabharata it's the word for
the battle formations armies took up, the best known being the *chakravyuha*, a spiral of soldiers.

We liked it because reporting has the same problem at a smaller scale. Sales sit in one file, invoices in another, and the
work is lining them up so you can see what's happening. Our logo shows that idea: three pieces forming around an open centre.

## How Vyuham works

### 1. Bring your data

Upload an Excel or CSV file of up to 10 MB and 1 million rows. Vyuham labels each column as a number, date, category or
text, and shows you a preview. If it reads a customer ID as a number, you can change the label, and later charts use your
correction.

If your data lives in a database, connect [PostgreSQL](https://www.postgresql.org/) or [MySQL](https://www.mysql.com/),
choose the tables you want, and set them to refresh every hour or every day.

### 2. Ask a question

Questions work best when they name what you want to measure and how to split it:

| You type | You get |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue by month | A line chart and a headline such as "Total revenue up 27% in Oct 2026" |
| Revenue by region | A bar chart and a headline such as "North leads with 48% of total revenue" |
| Top 5 products by revenue | A ranked bar chart of your five biggest products |
| Average amount by product | A bar chart showing which product has the highest average |
| How many orders in North | A single number, filtered to that region |
| Total revenue this year | A single number for the current year |

Vyuham matches your words to your column names, then runs a real query on your data. Every number on the screen comes from
that query. If it can't connect your question to a column, it tells you and suggests questions it can answer.

### 3. Build a dashboard and share it

Save any answer to a dashboard, or let Vyuham build a starter dashboard from your columns and edit from there. Each chart
carries a one-line headline, so you can read the page before you study it. A date filter applies across the dashboard.

When it's ready, invite teammates, create a read-only share link that you can turn off later, or export a PDF, CSV or Excel
copy. Alerts tell you when a number crosses a line, and scheduled reports send the dashboard on a set day.

### 4. Build what feeds it

When the raw data needs work before anyone asks about it, build that work in Vyuham too.

- **Pipelines.** Steps on a canvas: read a dataset, remove duplicates, run quality checks, transform rows in SQL and write a
  Bronze, Silver or Gold table. A pipeline runs on demand, hourly or daily in the background. Each step keeps a log, and you
  can retry only the steps that failed.
- **The AI Data Engineer.** Describe the pipeline in a sentence. It reads your real table and column names, drafts the
  steps, checks each one against your data and explains the design. Nothing it writes runs until a person approves it.
- **SQL Workbench.** Tabs, autocomplete, query plans, parameters and a cancel button, plus AI help to write, explain, fix or
  optimize a query. Every query is read-only and has a time limit.
- **Dynamic tables.** Save a query as a table and choose how fresh it should be. Vyuham works out what it depends on,
  refreshes upstream tables first, retries failures and records every refresh.

### 5. Decide who sees what

A scanner finds columns that look like emails, phone numbers, names, addresses, card numbers or government IDs. You label
them and choose a masking rule per role: partial, hashed, redacted or hidden. The query engine applies the rule, so a viewer
sees the masked value in a chart, an export, a shared link and an AI request alike. An audit log records who read, exported
or changed what. A workload report shows which queries and pipelines use the most time, with costs estimated from rates you
enter.

Your data lives in **Parquet files**, one folder per workspace. Each question opens a temporary **DuckDB** engine that can read only your folder, then closes. **PostgreSQL** holds accounts, dataset records and dashboards.

Anything on a schedule goes through **Redis** to a background **worker**. That's how connected tables refresh and alerts run while nobody is logged in. The worker reads your database in a read-only session and saves a copy, so your charts never add load to it.

## Who is Vyuham for?

Vyuham suits you if most of these sound familiar:

- Your numbers live in spreadsheets, exports or a single application database.
- One person spends hours each month copying data into a report.
- Cleaning and joining the data happens in scripts or cron jobs that only one person understands.
- You want several people to look at the same numbers without buying a license for each of them.
- Some columns, like customer emails, shouldn't be visible to everyone who reads a dashboard.

A shop owner tracking sales by product, a startup founder watching monthly revenue and an agency checking income by client
all fit that description. If you're still deciding whether this is worth your time, start with our guide to
[data analytics for small businesses](/blog/data-analytics-for-small-businesses).

## What Vyuham can't do yet

Knowing the limits will save you time, so here they are.

**Plain-English questions follow rules.** Asking on a dashboard doesn't use an AI model. It understands totals, averages,
counts, rankings, time periods and "by" breakdowns. It won't answer "why did revenue drop?" with a reason, and a broad
question such as "are we doing well?" needs rewording into something like "revenue by month". The AI features (SQL help and
the AI Data Engineer) work when AI is switched on for your workspace.

**Pipelines run SQL.** The AI also writes a pandas version of each step for you to export, but Vyuham runs the SQL.

**Incremental refresh is append-only.** It adds rows newer than a date or ID column, which suits tables that only grow.
Rows changed or deleted upstream show up after a full refresh.

**Costs are estimates.** Workload reports measure Vyuham's own work and multiply it by your rates. They aren't connected to a
cloud bill.

**Labels need to be consistent.** "Paid" and "PAID" count as two different values, so tidy those before you upload. A good
rule of thumb is one header row, one record per row and one date format per column.

**Google Sheets isn't connected, and there are no notebooks.** Download a sheet as Excel or CSV for now.

**Databases must be reachable from the internet.** Vyuham refuses private and local addresses, so a database that only
exists inside your office network won't connect.

## How is Vyuham different from Excel, Power BI or Airflow?

Excel is where a lot of good data starts, and you can keep using it. Vyuham reads the export and handles the charting and
the repeat reporting, so you stop rebuilding the same pivot table every month.

Power BI and Tableau do far more. They suit companies with someone who will own data models and maintain reports. If you
have that person, or you need custom measures across many systems, they're the better choice. Vyuham is for the team
without that person, or for the team that wants the building and the asking in one place.

Orchestrators such as Airflow, and tools such as dbt, go deeper on data engineering. Vyuham covers SQL pipelines on the data
in your workspace, with schedules, checks, lineage and retries built in. If you already run Airflow, Airbyte or cron, a
refresh hook lets them trigger Vyuham. There's a [side-by-side comparison on our homepage](/#compare).

| | Excel | Power BI or Tableau | Vyuham |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starting point | A blank sheet | A data model | Your existing file or table |
| How you ask | Formulas and pivots | DAX or calculated fields | A short plain-English question |
| Preparing data | By hand, each month | A separate tool or script | Pipelines and dynamic tables in the same workspace |
| Best for | Working on the data itself | Teams with an analyst | Teams that want asking and building in one place |
| Depth | Very high, by hand | Very high | Everyday business questions and SQL pipelines |

## Is my data safe in Vyuham?

Each company's data is kept in its own workspace, and a dashboard only becomes public if you create a share link for it.

For database connections, Vyuham opens read-only sessions, stores your password encrypted and asks for SSL by default. We
still recommend creating a separate database user that can only read the tables you want to analyze. Pipelines and dynamic
tables write to tables inside Vyuham, never to your database.

Masking rules and column access are applied in the query engine, as described above, and every export and permission change
is recorded in the audit log. You can read more on the [privacy page](/privacy).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to know SQL or formulas?

No. You can ask questions and build dashboards by typing short questions. If you do know SQL, the SQL Workbench gives you an
editor that can join an uploaded file with a table from your database, and pipelines and dynamic tables use SQL too.

### Can I build data pipelines without writing code?

Partly. Reading a dataset, removing duplicates, quality checks and writing a table are form fields on the canvas. Transforms
are SQL, which you can write yourself or ask the AI Data Engineer to draft for your approval.

### What file types does Vyuham accept?

Excel (.xlsx) and CSV files, up to 10 MB and 1 million rows each.

### Which databases can I connect?

PostgreSQL and MySQL, including MariaDB through the MySQL option. Managed services work the same way as long as they accept
SSL connections from the internet.

### Can Vyuham alert me when a number changes?

Yes. You can set an alert on a number that matters, such as weekly sales falling below a level, and schedule regular digests.
