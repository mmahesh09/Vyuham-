If you've searched for a way to turn spreadsheets into dashboards, Power BI was probably the first answer. It deserves to
be: it's one of the most capable reporting tools there is. The real question for a small team isn't which tool is more
powerful. It's who will build and look after the reports.

## The question that decides it

**Is there someone on your team whose job includes building reports?**

If yes, and they enjoy it, Power BI will give them almost unlimited control. If the honest answer is "the founder, on
Sunday evenings" or "whoever is least busy", the trade-offs look very different.

## Where the two differ

| | Power BI and similar tools | Vyuham |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Getting started | Build a data model first | Upload the file you already have |
| Who can use it | Someone trained in the tool | Anyone who can type a question |
| Calculations | DAX formulas and calculated fields | Plain English questions |
| What you get | Charts you then interpret | The answer in a sentence, then the chart |
| Pricing | Licensed per person | One price for the whole company |

## When Power BI is the better choice

We'd rather you pick the right tool than ours. Power BI is likely the better fit if:

- you have, or plan to hire, an analyst who will own reporting;
- you need to join many live systems (an ERP, a data warehouse and a CRM) into one model;
- your company already runs on Microsoft 365 and its admin and security tooling;
- you need highly customised visuals or row-level security across hundreds of users.

<Callout title="A useful test">
Write down the last five questions someone in your team asked about the numbers. If answering them needed new
relationships between tables or custom measures, that's Power BI territory. If they were "how did we do in March, and
which region dropped?", you don't need a data model to answer them.
</Callout>

## When Vyuham fits better

Vyuham is built for the team where the owner or a manager *is* the data team:

- your data is already in spreadsheets and exports, not a warehouse;
- you want answers this week, not a reporting project;
- several people should be able to ask questions, without a licence each;
- nobody wants to learn a formula language to find out what changed.

## You don't have to choose forever

Many teams start with spreadsheets, grow into a simple dashboard tool, and move to Power BI when they hire their first
analyst. If that's you, start with the tool you'll actually open on Monday morning. The best dashboard is the one
someone reads.
